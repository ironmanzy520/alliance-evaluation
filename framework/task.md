# Task definition and dimensions

The Task Core comprises three definitional elements:

- `agreement on the activities`;
- `activities perceived by both participants as relevant and efficacious`;
- `acceptance of responsibility to perform the activities`.

These elements are organised from Bordin (1979) and Horvath and Greenberg (1989). They are not three established subscales and do not form a total score. Goal agreement, technical correctness, symptom improvement and general satisfaction are not relabelled as Task Core evidence.

An Activity is specific therapeutic work explicitly mentioned or described in the dialogue. It may be proposed, selected, explained, negotiated, performed or reviewed. A problem, symptom, topic, goal, feeling or general disclosure does not itself establish an Activity. Work is registered before its dimensions are evaluated; episodes are not pre-segmented and the turn judge cannot add work to the registry. See the [Activity identification prompt](../prompts/identify_activity.txt).

The dimensions and Core elements use activity for specific work and Task only for the core construct. This project adaptation preserves the meanings of agreement, relevance/efficacy and responsibility. Citations explain source relationships, not inherited reliability or validity.

| Dimension | Evidence subject | Observation target | Final result |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Collaborative activity negotiation | Counsellor Contributor | Substantive client participation when specific work is selected, defined or changed, and how the client's input affects its handling | Turn result |
| Activity and responsibility clarification | Counsellor Contributor | Once work reaches an evaluable explanation or progression stage, clarity about what to do, how to do it and genuinely applicable responsibilities; simple work need not be over-explained | Turn result |
| Activity–client relevance linkage | Counsellor Contributor | An identifiable connection between specific work and the client's expressed circumstances; established rationale need not be repeated in later turns | Turn result |
| Activity-related responsiveness | Counsellor Contributor | Use of new client feedback that may affect the work to adjust it or continue it with a reasoned basis | Turn result |
| Activity agreement/disagreement | Dyadic Indicator | Both participants' positions on the same work or step, evaluated when the current turn makes their relationship assessable; either speaker may trigger a result | Turn result |
| Client-expressed activity relevance | Client Indicator | The client's explicit account of how the work relates to their difficulties, needs, goals or desired change | Turn result |
| Client-expressed activity efficacy | Client Indicator | The client's explicit view that the work may help, has helped, has not helped or remains uncertain; not clinical effectiveness | Turn result |
| Client engagement in the activity | Client Indicator | Text-observable client involvement in specific work; not equivalent to acceptance of responsibility | Summary for each registered Activity; client turns accumulate evidence only |

Full opportunity conditions and categories are in the [turn cards](../prompts/judge_turn.txt) and [summary cards](../prompts/judge_summary.txt). Reasons for absent ratings in the seven turn dimensions remain unknown; summary does not explain or fill them.

## Core mappings

These predefined conceptual correspondences organise saved dimension results. Relevance and efficacy are displayed separately but remain parts of the Core element concerning both participants' perceptions of relevance and efficacy.

| Dimension | Core element | Conceptual rationale |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Collaborative activity negotiation | agreement on the activities | Incorporating the client's views into Activity selection provides a basis for jointly deciding on specific work. |
| Activity and responsibility clarification | acceptance of responsibility to perform the activities | Clarifying the work and each participant's responsibilities provides conditions for understanding and accepting a commitment to it. |
| Activity–client relevance linkage | activities perceived as relevant | Connecting an Activity to the client's concerns provides a basis for judging its personal relevance. |
| Activity-related responsiveness | agreement on the activities | Incorporating new feedback relates to maintaining or renegotiating agreement on the work. |
| Activity agreement/disagreement | agreement on the activities | Connecting both participants' positions on the same Activity makes specific agreement or disagreement visible. |
| Client-expressed activity relevance | activities perceived as relevant | The client's account of the link between an Activity and their concerns supplies their perspective on relevance. |
| Client-expressed activity efficacy | activities perceived as efficacious | The client's account of expected or experienced help supplies their perspective on usefulness. |
| Client engagement in the activity | acceptance of responsibility to perform the activities | The study treats engagement as an indirect indication of responsibility acceptance, based on the relationship between participating in specific work and taking a role within it. |

## Source relationships and interpretation boundaries

The Core sources for agreement, relevance/efficacy and responsibility do not supply a ready-made online Activity segmentation algorithm. Negotiation, clarification and relevance linkage operationalise that theory and the collaboration/work-organisation sources listed under [Shared sources](README.md#shared-sources) and [Dimension sources](#dimension-sources). Responsiveness draws on Stiles, Honos-Webb and Surko (1998) and Ryum and Kazantzis (2024). Engagement adapts Eubanks and Muran (2022), WT 5, to text and individual Activities.

A counsellor's rationale does not establish that the client perceives the work as relevant or efficacious. Clarification, engagement and general compliance are not equivalent to responsibility acceptance. Brief acceptance clearly directed at a specific work proposal may provide agreement evidence; an ungrounded “okay” does not automatically do so. The current dimensions do not independently assess acceptance of responsibility.

Activity records remain separate and retain original turn order. Evidence is not borrowed across Activities, earlier changes of position are not overwritten, and ratings are not aggregated across Activities.
