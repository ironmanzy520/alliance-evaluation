# Bond definition and dimensions

The Bond Core comprises `mutual trust`, `acceptance` and `mutual respect`. Mutual trust and acceptance draw on Horvath and Greenberg (1989); the direct wording for mutual respect draws on Eubanks and Muran (2022), WT 2. These elements organise the study's definitional scope, not three independent rating dimensions.

The table outlines observation targets and result levels. Full opportunity conditions and allowed categories remain in the [turn cards](../prompts/judge_turn.txt) and [summary cards](../prompts/judge_summary.txt); this page introduces no additional rating anchors.

| Dimension | Evidence subject | Observation target | Final result |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Accurate Empathy | Counsellor Contributor | Accurate understanding of the client's current feelings or meaning, communicated in language that fits the current content; reflective wording alone does not establish accuracy | Sample summary; turns accumulate evidence only |
| Therapist accepts/validates Patient | Counsellor Contributor | A non-judgemental, supportive stance recognising the intelligibility of the client's experience; agreement with the client's views is not required | Sample summary; turns accumulate evidence only |
| Grounded content uptake | Counsellor Contributor | Substantive use of an identifiable earlier client contribution in the current response, preserving its meaning | Turn result |
| Client confirmation/disconfirmation | Client Indicator | Explicit confirmation, partial correction or rejection of a specific earlier counsellor understanding, not a general response | Turn result |
| Client-perceived acceptance | Client Indicator | The client's explicit perception of the counsellor's stance or their relationship, not inferred from general disclosure | Turn result |
| Mutual trust and respect | Dyadic Indicator | Connected evidence of trust, respect and reciprocity from both participants; unilateral contributions may be saved but do not establish mutuality | Sample summary; both participants' turns may contribute evidence |

## Core mappings

These predefined conceptual correspondences organise saved dimension results. Bond as a whole denotes no single Core element. Mapping the same Mutual trust and respect result to two elements does not create two independent ratings.

| Dimension | Core element or scope | Conceptual rationale |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Accurate Empathy | Bond as a whole | Understanding the client's experience may provide a basis for relational connection, linking this contribution to Bond as a whole. |
| Therapist accepts/validates Patient | acceptance | Recognising the intelligibility of the client's experience connects acceptance to how the counsellor treats the client. |
| Grounded content uptake | mutual respect | Preserving the client's intended meaning and respecting their interpretative authority motivates this study's link between content uptake and respect. |
| Client confirmation/disconfirmation | Bond as a whole | Confirming or challenging a specific understanding adds the client's perspective to the counsellor's empathic contribution. |
| Client-perceived acceptance | acceptance | The client's explicit perception connects acceptance to the recipient's experience of the counsellor's stance. |
| Mutual trust and respect | mutual trust; mutual respect | Connected responses from both participants make mutuality of trust and respect observable. |

## Source relationships and interpretation boundaries

- Accurate Empathy draws on Truax (1967), using a representative sample-level anchor under text-only conditions. It supplies Bond-level contribution evidence rather than representing a single Core element.
- Acceptance/validation and mutual trust/respect draw respectively on Eubanks and Muran (2022), WT 3 and WT 2. The source instrument's information conditions and observation units do not automatically transfer to text coding.
- Grounded content uptake is a project synthesis supported by relevant observation approaches in Hughes et al. (2025), Elliott et al. (2023) and Rautalinko et al. (2007). It inherits neither scores nor validity from any one source.
- Confirmation/disconfirmation draws on Elliott et al. (2023), primarily to corroborate or challenge understanding. Additional client information forms a category only when the specific relational conditions are met.
- Client-perceived acceptance draws on Rogers (1957/1992) and Barrett-Lennard (1962) and is coded only when the perception is explicitly expressed.

Counsellor behaviour may corroborate or conflict with client or dyadic evidence; these cannot be added together to prove Bond has formed. Continued responses, politeness, absence of objection and positive affect do not automatically constitute positive Indicators. Retaining six dimensions does not establish their independence, observability or validity.
